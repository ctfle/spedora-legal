Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 October 2026

Spedora is a cycling training app: you plan workouts, ride them on a smart trainer, and look back at your training. This page explains what personal data Spedora uses, why, who else gets it, and what you can do about it. Spedora has no ads, no analytics or tracking tools, and never sells your data.

Who is responsible

Christoph Fleckenstein, Tschamlerstraße 3, 6020 Innsbruck, Austria, is responsible for your data (the "controller" under the EU General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR). For anything about your data, write to support@spedora.app.

What Spedora stores, and why

  • Your account: the email address and name from the account you sign in with (Apple or Google), and an account id. Needed to give you an account.
  • Your profile: FTP, power and heart rate zones, maximum heart rate, units, and optionally your name, birth year and weight. Used to size your workouts and show your numbers.
  • Your training: workouts you build or save, your plan, the name, length, climb and elevation profile of routes you import (not the GPX file itself, see below), and rides you record: power, heart rate, cadence, speed, distance, duration and how the ride felt. This is what the app is for.
  • Your training profile: your answers to the AI Coach's questions (goals, time, experience, and anything you write in the free-text answer), the AI's summary of them, and the season outline the AI writes.
  • AI usage: for each AI request, the time, the model, how many tokens it used, what it cost and how long it took. Not what was asked or answered. Used to keep each account within its daily and monthly limits.
  • Error reports: when something goes wrong that could lose your data (such as a ride that couldn't be saved), the time, what failed and technical details such as sizes and error codes, never the ride itself. Used to find and fix the problem.
  • On your phone: a ride in progress is kept on the phone until it's saved, so it can be recovered after a crash; a copy of your plan, rides and profile, so the app works offline; changes and rides waiting to be sent; the language you picked; the trainer and heart rate sensors you've paired; and the GPX files of routes you import, so where a route goes never reaches Spedora's server. This stays on the device; the offline copy is removed when you sign out, the route files when you delete your account.

Heart rate, and anything you write about injuries or health in your answers, can be health data, which the GDPR protects specially (Article 9). Spedora records heart rate only with your explicit consent, asked when you first pair a heart rate strap; without it, rides are recorded without heart rate. It uses health data only to show and plan your own training, sends it to the AI Coach only with your separate consent (see below), and never uses it for anything else or shares it beyond what this page lists.

Legal basis

  • Your account, profile, training, and syncing to apps you connect: to provide the app you signed up for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Heart rate: your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time in your profile, under Account; Spedora then stops recording heart rate. To have it removed from rides already recorded, delete those rides or write to the address above.
  • The AI Coach: your consent, which the app asks for before the first AI request (Art. 6(1)(a) and, for health data, Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). Your training history, with its heart rate, goes only with your explicit consent too, asked when you first turn on "Share my training history"; turn it off to stop sending it for a plan. You can withdraw either consent at any time in your profile, under Account. That stops further requests; it doesn't undo ones already made.
  • The AI usage log and limits: Spedora's legitimate interest in preventing abuse and keeping costs in check (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Error reports: Spedora's legitimate interest in keeping the app working and your rides safe (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

The AI Coach (Claude by Anthropic)

The AI Coach runs on Claude, an AI made by Anthropic PBC in the USA. Only when you use it (when you answer its questions, or ask it for a plan) does Spedora send Anthropic:

  • For the assessment: your answers, including the free-text answer, plus your FTP, weight and age (worked out from your birth year; the birth year itself isn't sent).
  • For a plan: your training profile (answers, summary, level, season outline), FTP, power zones, your planned workouts and routes, and what you type to the AI Coach.
  • If you turn on "Share my training history": your rides of the last four weeks too, summarised as time in each zone, heart rate, how each ride felt, and each ride's day, length and whether it was hard; and your maximum heart rate (the one in your profile, or else one estimated from your birth year; the birth year itself isn't sent).

Each request also says which language you use the app in, so the AI Coach answers in it. Your name and email address are not sent. Anthropic processes this data for Spedora under its commercial terms and data processing agreement, does not use it to train its models, and keeps it only for a limited time. As the data goes to the USA, the transfer is covered by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses in that agreement.

Hosting

Spedora's database, sign-in and server functions are hosted by Supabase in the EU (Ireland), which processes the data for Spedora under a data processing agreement. Supabase is a US company; the EU Standard Contractual Clauses in that agreement cover any access from outside the EU. Supabase records technical logs (such as IP addresses) for security, kept for a short time.

Apps you connect

Spedora only shares data with these when you connect them, and only what's needed:

  • Strava and intervals.icu: rides you upload to them. Spedora keeps the access they grant on its server, never in the app. Their own privacy policies apply to what they then do with it.
  • Apple Health: Spedora writes your rides (duration, distance, active energy) to Apple Health on your phone, if you allow it. It doesn't read anything from it.
  • Sign-in with Apple or Google: they tell Spedora who you are; their own privacy policies apply to the sign-in.

How long data is kept

Your data is kept as long as you have an account. You can delete single workouts, rides, routes and your training profile (Delete on the training profile) at any time. Deleting your account (Profile, Account, Delete Account) deletes your account and everything Spedora stores for it, including the AI usage log and error reports, and disconnects Strava and intervals.icu. Data already uploaded to apps you connected stays there until you delete it there.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • get a copy of your data and know how it's used (Art. 15), and receive it in a portable form (Art. 20),
  • have it corrected (Art. 16) or deleted (Art. 17), or its use restricted (Art. 18),
  • object to its use based on legitimate interest (Art. 21),
  • withdraw your consent at any time, without affecting what was done before (Art. 7(3)),
  • complain to a data protection authority (Art. 77): in Austria the Datenschutzbehörde (dsb.gv.at), or the one where you live.

In the app you can correct your profile, delete single workouts, rides and routes or your whole account, and withdraw your consent to heart rate recording, to the AI Coach and to sharing your training history. For a copy of your data, or anything else, write to support@spedora.app.

Changes

If Spedora starts using your data in a new way, this page will be updated first, and the app will ask again where your consent is needed.
